02 · The transformation

Six structural chapters of India's steel story

The report itself frames the journey around state-led capacity creation, liberalisation and deregulation, private-sector expansion, global competitiveness and the move toward technology and sustainability.

01
1947 → 1961

Start with a small base

India entered the Independence era with a very small steel base. The first phase centred on building the foundations for a national steel industry through planned industrialisation.

02
1962 → 1976

Build national capacity

Large integrated public-sector plants at Bhilai, Durgapur, Rourkela and Bokaro expanded the national steelmaking footprint and pushed production toward a much larger scale.

03
1977 → 1991

Consolidate & expand

The public-sector-led steel ecosystem matured, with integrated capacity, supporting infrastructure and domestic demand forming the base for the next structural transition.

04
1992 → 2006

Liberalise the sector

Public-sector reservation was reduced, licensing was withdrawn subject to locational restrictions, and pricing and distribution controls ended. Private participation expanded.

05
2007 → 2021

Compete & modernise

The sector moved beyond capacity creation toward competitiveness, technology, raw-material security, R&D, productivity, quality and investment, supported by the National Steel Policy framework.

06
2022 → 2026

Scale, specialise & modernise

Specialty steel, digitisation, logistics, R&D, recycling, safety and technology initiatives are being layered onto an expanding production and capacity base.

07 · Technology transition

Three steelmaking routes — one evolving production system

BOF · Basic Oxygen Furnace

41.1%

Largest reported route in FY2024–25. Oxygen is blown through hot metal to refine it into crude steel.

EAF · Electric Arc Furnace

20.8%

Electricity creates the arc that melts the charge. EAF enables flexible steelmaking, including greater use of recycled metallics.

Induction Furnace

38.2%

A major part of India's route mix, using electromagnetic induction to heat and melt metallic charge inside the furnace.

Digitisation is also moving upstream

The Ministry describes digitisation as an important element for optimising mining. A roadmap has been put in place for iron-ore mining, with digitalisation being adopted across operations to improve productivity, safety, transparency and logistics performance.

10 · The intellectual takeaway

What the numbers really say

Scale

India moved from about 1 MT of steel capacity at Independence to more than 160 MT of annual crude-steel production today.

Structure

The industry moved from an entirely private pre-Independence base, through public-sector-led nation building, to a predominantly private-sector production ecosystem.

Capability

The agenda has widened from tonnes and capacity to specialty steel, R&D, digital mines, resource efficiency, recycling, logistics and low-carbon technologies.

The 2047 lens

India has built a globally significant steel industry since Independence. The next question is: what kind of metals ecosystem will it build by 2047?
